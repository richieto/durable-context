First decision

Use durable context when the reasoning is expensive to lose

Durable Context is opt-in. It is for meaningful work whose intent, trade-offs, delivery consequences, or architectural decisions must survive sessions, agents, and handoffs.

Use native planning

Small, routine, reversible

The change is well understood, locally contained, and cheap to revisit. Ship it without creating an initiative.

or
Use Durable Context

Meaningful, ambiguous, consequential

Intent needs clarification, several concerns interact, decisions are costly to reverse, or another human or agent must continue.

The goal is not more documentation. The goal is enough durable reasoning to continue correctly without preserving chat history.

Allocation, not methodology

Put each initiative in one current cycle

A cycle is a generic namespace. Your project may treat it as a release, sprint, milestone, quarter, or any other meaningful cadence. Durable Context does not add dates, goals, capacity, scheduling, or cycle status.

The working journey

Move from unclear intent to a durable outcome

Invoke durable-context with a named initiative. The front door resumes recorded state and advances only to the next meaningful boundary.

1

Clarify

Separate repository facts from the human choices that shape intent.

2

Plan

Settle goals, success, scope, constraints, options, and open questions.

3

Challenge

Optionally steelman and pressure-test a meaningful recommendation.

4

Dive

Route material concerns and interrogate only decision-bearing gaps.

5

Deliver

Implement, verify, checkpoint, and disclose transferred follow-ups.

6

Distill

Promote self-contained decisions and released-behavior knowledge.

Planning boundary

Direction is clear enough to review or design.

Detailed-design boundary

Every material concern has an owner, route, and settled implication.

PR boundary

Evidence, decisions, and follow-up dispositions tell the final truth.

When intent is the unknown

Interview the human instead of guessing

Repository evidence answers what exists. It cannot decide what outcome the human values, which trade-off is acceptable, or whether the emerging interpretation is actually the intended one.

Intent interview

Clarify before recommending

  • State the ambiguity and why it changes the work.
  • Ask one decision-bearing question or one small group at a time.
  • Offer concrete options when they help expression, without assuming one.
  • Reflect the emerging interpretation back for correction.
  • Stop when direction is clear enough for the next boundary.
Do not interrogate by default

Inspect first, ask selectively

  • Do not ask questions the repository can answer.
  • Do not transfer routine implementation choices to the human.
  • Do not turn every concern into a questionnaire.
  • Do not use challenge to invent a target when intent is unsettled.
  • Record conclusions and open questions—not the transcript.

Planning interviews for goals and boundaries. Dive interviews for material concern ambiguity. Challenge first steelmans a settled recommendation, then uses focused questions to expose assumptions and alternatives.

One front door, focused methods

Remember durable-context; reach for specialists deliberately

Skills are invocation-only and agent-agnostic Markdown instructions. They shape repository artifacts and workflow boundaries, while the host agent and the user's request still govern conversational style.

Skill Responsibility Human interaction
durable-context Resume state and choose the next safe boundary. Routes unclear intent back to planning.
plan-with-context Settle direction and maintain the integration plan. Intent interview when goals, scope, or trade-offs are unclear.
challenge Steelman and pressure-test a meaningful recommendation. Clarifies the target, then exposes assumptions and alternatives.
dive-into-plan Route and resolve material concern-level consequences. Asks only questions that can change a concern's design or route.
backfill-with-context Reconstruct evidence when ordinary work grows large. Separates observed facts, confirmed intent, inference, and unknowns.
checkpoint-context Validate lifecycle consistency and record continuity. Stops at human choices and records one concrete next action.

Project-profile baseline and refresh remain advanced maintenance skills for stable repository facts. Small changes continue to use normal agent planning without Durable Context.

Artifact quality

Write the minimum sufficient record

Completeness is semantic, not a word-count target. Another human or agent should be able to continue correctly without reading the original conversation.

Keep

Information that changes continuation

  • The settled conclusion or current direction.
  • Decision-relevant rationale, constraints, and accepted trade-offs.
  • Evidence or a stable link to the artifact that owns it.
  • Unresolved material questions, risks, and one concrete next action.
Omit

Volume without durable value

  • Conversation transcripts and narration of routine work.
  • Generic engineering advice or exhaustive hypothetical branches.
  • Repository facts already owned elsewhere.
  • Repeated conclusions copied into every document.
README.md

Compact state, routes, open material questions, and next action.

plan.md

Integration map: direction, scope, options, sequencing, and links.

Concern documents

Only independently material detail, stored once in its owning file.

backlog.md

Bounded implementation state inside this initiative—not project planning.

follow-up.md

Final disclosure of completed, transferred, or human-waived work.

Root ADR

Self-contained accepted decision with stable commit, PR, or release provenance.

Expand detail in proportion to uncertainty, impact, risk, and cost of reversal. A concise No impact — reason or explicit Unknown is better than defensive filler.

Lifetime determines location

Distill before deleting disposable context

The initiative and its cycle may disappear. Anything required to understand an accepted decision or released behavior must stand on its own outside that working memory.

Disposable working memory

context/

  • Plans, interviews, alternatives, and open questions.
  • Challenge records, backfill evidence, and checkpoints.
  • Initiative backlog and follow-up disclosures.
  • Optional release-documentation hints.
Must survive deletion

decisions/ and reference/

  • Accepted architectural and design conclusions.
  • Enough rationale and constraints to understand why.
  • Stable commit, PR, release, or source provenance.
  • Released behavior explained without context links as dependencies.
Before deleting a closed cycle
Check that accepted decisions and shipped-behavior reference are self-contained and that transferred work has a stable destination.
context/ can then disappear without erasing durable truth.
